What I Owned

I built Glikit, the Unity authoring toolset for actors, cameras, objectives, flags, sequencers, and Yarn nodes. I also worked on the runtime coordination behind dialogue scenes and on the menu, save-slot, checkpoint, and cross-scene restoration flow.

Featured work

Three selected parts of my work on Psycorp.

01 / Featured work

Giving designers one place to build a scene

As more dialogue moments and actors were added, setting up each scene by hand made missing references easy to overlook. A scene could work in isolation and still be hard for another designer to repeat.

I built Glikit around reusable definitions and validation for actors, flags, objectives, sequencers, camera setups, and Yarn nodes. The point was to catch setup problems while authoring and make the same workflow usable across scenes.

02 / Featured work

Connecting Yarn, actors, and cameras at runtime

Dialogue had to do more than display lines. It needed to stage actors, change shots, and update objectives without every Yarn command reaching into a different scene object.

I used stable actor IDs, scene configurations, camera anchors, and runtime coordinators to connect Yarn commands to game services. That made an authored sequence capable of changing the scene while keeping the staging rules in one pipeline.

03 / Featured work

Restoring a scene without replaying its events

Saving a narrative game is more than remembering the player position. A loaded scene also needs the right flags, objectives, actors, objects, and camera state, without firing earlier events again.

I tied save slots and checkpoints to stable IDs and an ordered restoration flow. Global state is restored before the scene rebuilds its visible state, so players can resume from the menu into a coherent narrative moment.

Supporting contributions

Project takeaway

Psycorp changed the way I think about narrative implementation. Once several people author content, a working scene is only one part of the job; the authoring rules and restoration order have to be understandable and repeatable too.
