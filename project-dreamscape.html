What I Owned

I balanced Followers, Chargers, and Golems around distinct combat jobs. I adjusted health, damage, stagger, detection, and attack timing, checked readability with design and QA, and documented the reasons for each pass.

Featured work

Three selected parts of my work on Project Dreamscape.

01 / Featured work

Giving each enemy a job in the encounter

The enemies functioned, but that did not guarantee they created different pressure. I defined Followers as steady crowd pressure, Chargers as burst disruption, and Golems as area control, then judged their tuning against those jobs.

That gave balancing passes a useful question: what decision should the player make when this enemy appears? Health, movement, reach, and stagger only mattered if they reinforced that decision.

02 / Featured work

Tuning pressure and counterplay

Followers were disappearing too quickly to keep pressure on a group fight, so I raised their health and speed and narrowed their damage range. They could stay present without relying on unpredictable spikes.

Chargers had the opposite problem: burst damage and weak telegraphing made hits feel arbitrary, while a long charge disrupted pacing. I lowered their base damage range and charge duration, then increased detection distance to keep their disruptive role while giving players more time to respond.

03 / Featured work

Using testing to make the Golem hold space

Golems were too easy to interrupt, so their area attacks did not force players to reposition. I increased health, smash force, and the dazed threshold to make them a steadier spatial threat.

Across these passes I used design and QA feedback to check telegraphs, time to kill, and recovery windows, then recorded the tuning rationale for the next iteration. The goal was readable pressure, not simply stronger enemies.

Supporting contributions

  • Health, damage, and stagger balancing
  • Detection and response-window tuning
  • Mixed encounter readability checks
  • Combat tuning documentation

Project takeaway

Dreamscape reminded me that a stat change is only useful when it changes a player decision. I learned to describe enemy behavior in terms of pressure and counterplay first, then use numbers and testing to support that role.
