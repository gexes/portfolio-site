A high-speed, bowling-inspired trip through the afterlife. I designed level flow and built spline, checkpoint, and direction systems that help fast traversal stay readable.

What I Owned

I led gameplay feature iteration across design sprints, built the guiding-light mechanic and checkpoint recovery, and programmed wrong-way feedback from spline orientation. I also developed a repeatable spline-based approach to track layout.

Featured work

Three selected parts of my work on Charon's Corner.

01 / Featured work

Designing spaces for a fast controller

Drifting and velocity chaining only feel good when the track gives players time to read the next turn. A path that looked clear in the editor could become confusing once the player reached it at speed.

I treated speed, sightlines, slopes, and obstacles as linked design constraints. Elevation changes and physical “pins” made a basic route more active while keeping the intended direction visible.

02 / Featured work

Making track construction repeatable

A single successful level was not enough for an 80-person team. The team needed rules that could produce more tracks without turning each section into a custom setup.

I built layouts from cascading spline paths so slope transitions and directional flow could be shaped consistently. The framework gave level designers a starting structure, while changes in elevation and obstacles kept the path from becoming a flat “snake way.”

03 / Featured work

Helping players recover and reorient

At this speed, a missed turn or fall can cost the player their sense of direction. I added checkpoint recovery to return the player to a known position, plus a guiding light as a moving point of interest.

The wrong-way system compares the player’s orientation with the spline direction and presents UI feedback when they face away from the objective. Together these systems support experimentation without making recovery confusing.

Supporting contributions

  • Gameplay feature review and iteration
  • Guiding-light implementation
  • Checkpoint coordinate tracking
  • Wrong-way UI feedback
  • Obstacle and elevation passes

Project takeaway

Charon’s Corner made me design the track as part of the controller. The spline gave the team a repeatable construction method, but the level only worked when slope, obstacle placement, recovery, and guidance all respected the player’s speed.
